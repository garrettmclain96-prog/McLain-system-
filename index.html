      Opening scene · first draft

      The water came in under the door the way bad news does: quietly at first, then all at once. By the time he noticed it, it had already found the low corner of the room and filled it, dark and patient, and it was reaching for the next one.

      He did not look at the water. He looked at the candle.

      It stood on the only table in the room, a stub of wax no taller than his thumb, and its flame leaned toward him the way a child leans toward the person who has come to pick him up. He had carried it through three moves and a winter without heat. He had shielded it with his hands in parking lots. He knew the exact sound it made before it went out, a small dry tick, and he had learned to hear it through anything.

      The water reached his ankles. It was warm, which was worse.

      He picked the candle up and held it against his chest.

      On the walls hung the things he had built, each in a plain frame: a drawing of a gate, a map with seven counties circled, a hand-lettered sign for a business that did not exist yet. The water climbed toward them without hurry. He saw that the frames stayed dry. The water rose and the frames stayed dry, as if the room had decided which things it was allowed to take.

      When it reached his knees he said, out loud, to no one, “Not this one.”

      The flame ticked. He cupped his hand around it and it steadied, and in its light he saw what he had not seen before: a door in the far wall, above the waterline. It had not been there a moment ago. Or it had been there the whole time, waiting for him to stop looking down.

      He started walking.
